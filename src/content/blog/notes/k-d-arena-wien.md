# Kruder & Dorfmeister concert in Arena Wien

So I went to the K&D concert in the Arena Wien last Thursday (Aug 20), and what should I say, it was just amazing. A series of backflashes to my youth.

The occasion: **"K+D KICKS 30"** — exactly 30 years after their legendary *DJ-Kicks* mix album came out on !K7 in March 1996. The Arena Open Air was sold out, doors at 19:00, and the duo delivered an audiovisual anniversary show with a seriously impressive visual production behind them.

## 30-year-old music from the future

What amazed me most was how music from 30 years ago still sounded futuristic. The dubby downbeat, the lazy hip-hop swing, those dubbed-out chords — none of it has dated a day. If anything, it sounded *more* contemporary than half of what gets released today. That's the thing about K&D: they weren't chasing a trend, they were building a sound so precisely their own that time never caught up with it.

## How two Viennese DJs put Austria on the map

Some context on why this anniversary matters. In the mid-90s, while Bristol had its trip-hop scene, Vienna suddenly became one of the global hot spots of electronic club music — and Peter Kruder & Richard Dorfmeister were the main reason why:

- Their **G-Stoned EP** (1993) with "High Noon" — plus that famous Simon-&-Garfunkel-style cover — broke through via UK tastemakers like Gilles Peterson, rare for a continental act at the time.
- Remix requests piled up from everyone: Madonna, Depeche Mode, Bomb the Bass, Roni Size, Lamb... their slow-motion dub reworks basically became a genre of their own.
- ***The K&D Sessions*** (1998) went on to sell over a million copies and was playing in every boutique and hair salon for seasons.
- Side projects **Tosca** (Dorfmeister) and **Peace Orchestra** (Kruder) kept pushing the sound, and their label **G-Stone** built a whole roster around it — always independent, always on their own clock.

For a few years there, the "Viennese school" of downtempo — K&D, Tosca, Waldeck, Sofa Surfers, dZihan & Kamien — was an export hit. Two guys from Vienna standing center stage of world electronic music: that hadn't really happened before.

And then hearing it all again, live, 30 years later, in their hometown, at the Arena of all places — with visuals to match — made the "still sounds like the future" feeling land even harder.
